# Goal

Keeping the record costs less than the mistakes it prevents, for somebody who
is not its author and has no stake in it succeeding.

# Why

**This goal is written late, and the lateness is the point.** For twenty-one
rounds the only goal on record was
[G0001](G0001-a-brain-others-can-adopt.md) — *a brain someone else can adopt
without being told how* — which measures whether the thing installs, not
whether it was worth installing. Asked directly whether the brain is worth
using, the honest answer was that the question was partly unanswerable,
because **you cannot fail at a goal you never stated.**

That is the failure D0001 exists to prevent, sitting in the goal layer of the
project built to prevent it, for five months.

Adoption is now broadly met and value is not measured. One adopter said
plainly: *"Worth it, but almost none of the value came from the parts the
model is built around."* A statement like that is the most useful thing in the
record and it had nothing to be checked against.

# Signals

Numbers that already exist, so this is not aspirational from day one:

- **Ceremony stays under 10% per change.** Measured at 15–25% by a consulting
  adopter before `bin/note` and `bin/which`, and ~5% after — though they
  attributed the drop to the record having pre-framed the question rather than
  to either tool, and the cost **relocated to retrieval** rather than
  disappearing. Retrieval grows with the record where classification is flat
  per item, so this signal gets harder with age, which is correct.
- **A tool the operator reaches for unprompted.** `bin/relevant` has been named
  *"the best tool per keystroke"* by one adopter and used unprompted on real
  work by another. `constraints`, `ancestry`, `stale`, `reconcile` and the task
  graph went unused in at least one corpus each. **A tool nobody reaches for is
  a cost, not a feature**, and the honest response to a persistent one is
  deletion.
- **A mistake the record demonstrably prevented, named by somebody else.**
  Three exist: a 24-versus-48-hour cancellation policy caught before it reached
  every family, a direct PDF link that would have silently broken
  acknowledgement tracking, and a second mislabelled notification turned into
  *"this is a pattern"*. All three came from an append-only prose journal, not
  from the typed graph.
- **The record is consulted when it is inconvenient.** Supersede discipline
  mattered most to one adopter on a day they were wrong three times and the
  wrong versions stayed visible. A record only read when it agrees with you is
  not being used.
- **Somebody keeps it for a quarter without being asked to.** The oldest brain
  here is months old. Nothing has yet survived long enough for staleness to be
  the dominant risk, which is the condition under which all of the above gets
  genuinely hard.

# What would disprove this

An adopter who keeps the record faithfully for a quarter and cannot name a
mistake it prevented. That is a cheaper outcome to discover than it looks, and
it has not been tested — every corpus here is younger than the question.
